# Equity Ownership and Share Repurchase Analysis

### Ownership Structure

**Methodology:** The 1% threshold is calculated based on total economic ownership rather than voting power. As of March 31, 2026, Agora had 338.2 million ordinary shares outstanding. Because each ADS represents four Class A ordinary shares, the 1% threshold is approximately 3.38 million ordinary shares, or 845,500 ADSs. The company's Form 20-F provides comprehensive disclosure for shareholders owning more than 5% and for directors and executive officers. For outside shareholders in the 1%–5% range, the analysis must rely on sources such as Forms 13F and 13G, BNY ADR/FactSet data, and MarketScreener. Accordingly, this is a list of **visible 1%+ holders**, not a guaranteed exhaustive shareholder register.

#### How Long Have These Shareholders Held Their Positions?

Tony Zhao is the founder and has held his position since before the IPO. The Class B control structure has remained in place since the listing: the 2021 annual report showed him holding all 76.18 million Class B shares, and he continued to hold all Class B shares in 2026. There has been no meaningful dilution of his voting control.

5Y/Morningside/Evolution are also legacy pre-IPO holders. In the 2021 annual report, Morningside-related entities held 47.98 million Class A shares, or 10.4% of the company. The position was still 47.98 million Class A shares, or 13.1%, in 2024, before declining to 32.13 million Class A shares, or 9.5%, in 2026. This indicates that the group is not new capital. It is an early-stage VC investor that had held the position for at least five years and began net selling over the past two years.

Susquehanna/SIG-related entities are not new capital either. SIG-related entities were already major shareholders in the 2021 annual report. A 2022 Schedule 13G reported approximately 27.54 million Class A shares, compared with 24.72 million Class A shares in the latest 2026 Schedule 13G/A. The group has held the position for at least four years and most likely entered before or around the IPO.

HHLR/Hillhouse was still a 5%+ holder in 2024, with 19.29 million Class A shares. Its 1Q 2026 Form 13F reported only 2.10 million ADSs, equivalent to approximately 8.39 million ordinary shares. This indicates at least two years of ownership, but also a significant reduction in exposure.

For Yunqi, Allspring, Neumann, Morgan Stanley, and UBS, the currently visible data comes mainly from BNY/FactSet, Forms 13F, and MarketScreener. Their holding periods are less traceable than those of 5Y, SIG, and HHLR. The more reasonable interpretation is that they are public-markets investors or dynamically managed positions, rather than strategic pre-IPO capital.

#### Whose Shares Is the Company Most Likely Buying Through Its Ongoing Repurchase Program?

The company has not disclosed its repurchase counterparties, so it is impossible to prove that it bought shares from any specific shareholder. Still, the available evidence supports a probability-based inference. Agora began repurchasing shares in early 2022 under a board-authorized program initially totaling $200 million, which has since been extended several times. As of March 31, 2026, the company had repurchased approximately 174.7 million Class A ordinary shares, equivalent to approximately 43.7 million ADSs, for approximately $156.2 million. Over the same period, total shares outstanding declined from approximately 449.8 million as of January 31, 2022, to 338.2 million.

The most likely primary source was not any one major shareholder, but the public float: smaller institutional holders, quantitative funds, trading accounts, and selling in the ADR market. The logic is straightforward. Cumulative repurchases of 43.7 million ADSs are significantly larger than the current position of most individual institutions. The company bought shares in the open ADS/Class A market rather than through a publicly disclosed targeted acquisition from a named holder.

If we must identify named legacy holders whose selling may have been partly absorbed by the company, three groups stand out.

**First, HHLR.** Its position declined from 19.29 million ordinary shares in 2024 to approximately 8.39 million ordinary shares in 2026, a reduction of roughly 10.9 million ordinary shares, or approximately 2.72 million ADSs. This reduction overlaps closely with the period during which Agora was actively repurchasing shares, so it is plausible that the company absorbed part of that selling through the market.

**Second, 5Y/Morningside/Evolution.** Its position declined from 47.98 million Class A shares in 2024 to 32.13 million Class A shares in 2026, a reduction of approximately 15.85 million shares, or 3.96 million ADSs. As an early-stage VC investor, the group's exit incentives are stronger than those of a typical mutual fund. If the selling occurred through the secondary market, Agora may have absorbed part of it through its repurchase program.

**Third, Susquehanna/SIG-related liquidity.** The position declined from approximately 27.54 million Class A shares in 2022 to 24.72 million Class A shares in 2026, a reduction of approximately 2.82 million shares, or roughly 0.70 million ADSs. The amount is not large, but its broker-dealer and trading-oriented profile makes it more likely to sit on both sides of market liquidity. Agora may therefore have indirectly bought shares released by SIG-related entities, their clients, or trading accounts.

**Key inference:** Agora's repurchases were primarily absorbing selling pressure from the public float that had built up during the prolonged post-listing weakness. A secondary source was the periodic exit of early-stage VCs and China-focused equity funds. The company was not buying the founder's control stock. If the main sources of potential selling pressure are ranked, the order is roughly: 5Y/Morningside/Evolution, HHLR, flexible capital such as Yunqi and Neumann, and Susquehanna's trading-related liquidity. Tony Zhao is not a major source of current selling pressure.
